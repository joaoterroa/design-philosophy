# Rendered comparison rubric

Review anonymous left/right pairs at equal scale before reading design rationale, the condition key, skill instructions or the other reviewer's conclusions. Screenshots are evidence of the rendered interface, not of how usable it feels in a real task. Inspect the live task path where practical and state what remains untested.

Use these six dimensions, with an example for each judgment:

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Composition | Does the arrangement create a clear whole? Do grouping, proportions and rhythm help the task? |
| Hierarchy | Does visual emphasis match what the person needs first, including a pending handover, SEV1 owner, stale feed or code error? |
| Task fit and usability | Can the person understand status, find actions, recover and retain needed information at the required size? |
| Product-specific personality | Does the interface have a coherent character suited to its product, beyond a conventional layout with a palette change? Quiet precision can be distinctive. |
| Craftsmanship | Are type, spacing, alignment, shape, content clearance and state transitions well related? Are awkward sizes and content handled? |
| Overall design quality | Which complete result best balances the previous dimensions without trading away the task? |

Scores:1weak,2material weaknesses,3competent,4strong,5exceptional. These are ordinal editorial judgments, not measured performance. Do not reward extra cards, larger curves, minimalism, unusual interactions, a specific font or resemblance to Dia on their own. A tie or uncertain preference is valid.

For each repeat, record left/right/same overall preference, confidence, dimension scores, concrete strengths and defects, and any task-critical risk. For the task, compare the two repeats: is the preference stable or mainly one lucky run? What would need a human or a real device?

Separate visible defects from inferred usability. Do not infer implementation correctness from a screenshot, full accessibility from a contrast number, or personality from compliance with an unseen design rule.
